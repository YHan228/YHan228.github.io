abstract: 'Background: Post-tuberculosis lung disease (PTLD), characterised by chronic respiratory symptoms and irreversible structural damage, affects up to half of Tuberculosis (TB) survivors, yet its genetic basis remains largely unknown.
Methods: We performed the first genome-wide association studies (GWASs) of lung function among people with TB in Africa: a cross-sectional GWAS at TB treatment completion (n=759) and a longitudinal GWAS of lung function trajectories during the first year after treatment initiation (n=918), applying a genome-wide significance threshold of P<5×10-8. Lung function was assessed using spirometric measures of forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1), forced vital capacity (FVC), and FEV1/FVC. Putative effector genes were identified using in silico prioritisation. Gene expression was examined in blood (bulk transcriptomics) and lung tissue (single-cell RNA sequencing) from people with TB. GWAS signals were further evaluated for associations with chest X-ray abnormalities, lung impairment patterns, and time to recovery.
Results: The cross-sectional and longitudinal GWASs identified two and four independent genome-wide significant signals, respectively. All six signals were distinct from previously reported lung-function variants. Gene prioritisation highlighted LAMA2, FHIT, and CARMIL1, all expressed in TB blood and lung tissue. Blood expression of FHIT and CARMIL1 decreased with greater lung impairment severity. Cross-sectional signals were associated with lung function recovery: C-allele carriers at rs73599041 had delayed recovery (HR=0.46; P=0.013), whereas C-allele carriers at rs6765946 recovered faster (HR=1.39; P=0.015).
Interpretation: Host genetics shapes post-TB repair processes, with LAMA2, FHIT, and CARMIL1 pointing to roles in matrix remodelling, epithelial integrity, and inflammatory signalling.'
